# alexandria, plainly.

The better your AI knows you, the better it can help you. When it remembers your work, what you care about, and how you like to think, every answer gets more useful. Every AI company knows this. That is why they are all building memory.

Now imagine that at full depth. Not a short list of facts about your life, but a detailed written mirror of how you think: what you believe, how you reason, what you are building, what you want to keep, and the questions you keep returning to. One mirror, owned by you, read by every AI you choose.

You do not have to wait for built-in memory to reach that depth. You can start today, free, in about five minutes.

Here is what happens. We provide a set of instructions. You give them to your own AI. The AI reads them, decides what fits, and changes how it works. It creates plain files on your computer and automatically writes what it learns about you, in as much detail as it can. Later, it reads those files whenever they help. Piece by piece, your AI builds the most detailed mirror of you that has ever existed.

The mirror is yours. Open every file. Read every line. Edit anything or delete it all. You choose which AIs can read it and how much they see. Their built-in memories keep working; the mirror sits beside them. If you move to a new AI, the mirror moves with you instead of making the next company work you out again.

Most of the loop happens on its own. Use AI as you already do, and it quietly writes what it learns. Before it helps again, it quietly reads back the parts that matter. You do not maintain a profile or fill in forms.

Your AI still cannot read your mind. For the things only you know, it can ask. Turn on a deliberate session and it asks what it has been wondering about you, points to gaps or contradictions, and pushes until you have said what you actually think. You just talk. It writes the result into the same files.

The first difference is immediate: the answers are actually for you. You stop explaining the same background in every new chat. An idea from one conversation can return in another. A new AI can pick up where the last one stopped.

The larger difference grows over time. Without the mirror, a useful thought helps once and gets buried. The next conversation cannot build on it. With the mirror, your AI can bring that thought back, improve it, connect it to new work, and help you do something because of it. You retain what matters, develop it, and act on it instead of letting it fade.

That changes the AI’s job. It still answers questions, but it can also develop the person asking them: challenge weak arguments, show connections you missed, hold you to aims you chose, and keep the evidence of how your thinking changed. AI thinks with you, not instead of you.

That is why this exists. It is about to become very easy to let machines do all the thinking. A mind you stop using fades. The opposite path is to use the same machines to think more, become more yourself, and keep the result under your control.

The obvious worries are covered. Your private mirror stays on your computer; our server has no endpoint that accepts it. Everything you already use keeps working. The files are ordinary markdown, not a format we control. Delete the folder and the loop is gone.

Alexandria is not another AI, app, or entity acting on you. “Alexandria loop” is simply our name for the pattern your own AI runs: write what it learns about you to files you own, then read those files when they help. The instructions are guidance, not hard rules. Your AI chooses how to apply them.

We give away the complete loop our founder uses. Start with nothing and it is a full setup. Already have a CLAUDE.md, memory files, notes, a vault, or your own loop? Keep them. Your AI moulds the instructions into what already works and takes only the pieces that help.

Start now for one reason: the mirror is made from time spent thinking with it. It cannot be back-filled in a weekend. Every conversation you keep gives every better model that comes later more of you to work with. The difference between starting today and meaning to start cannot be bought back later.

The free loop is complete by itself. The connector is optional, and it is the only paid part. Add it if you want compatible loops to learn from one another. You choose what to publish; your private files stay private. You can see what other people kept thinking about, how they developed it, what they made because of it, and which parts of their setup helped. The Library holds what people chose to share. The Marketplace holds the reusable parts of their loops.

Your loop stays yours and free. Alexandria runs the optional connector for people who want to learn and share across loops.

So the decision is small. If you want your AI to know you in full detail, if you want that knowledge in files you own, and if you want it helping you keep, develop, and act on what matters, start the loop. It is free. Five minutes from now, the first page of your mirror can be writing itself.
