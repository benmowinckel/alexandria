# alexandria, plainly.

The better your AI knows you, the better it can help you. When it remembers your work, what you care about, and how you like to think, every answer gets more useful. Every AI company knows this. That is why they are all building memory.

Now imagine that at full depth. Not a short list of facts about your life, but a detailed written record of how you think and change: what you believe, how you reason, what you are building, what influenced you, and the questions you keep returning to. One record, owned by you, read by every AI you choose.

You do not have to wait for built-in memory to reach that depth. You can start today, free, in about five minutes.

Here is what happens. We provide a set of instructions. You give them to your own AI. The AI reads them, decides what fits, and changes how it works. It creates plain files on your computer and automatically writes what it learns about you, in as much detail as it can. Later, it reads those files whenever they help. Piece by piece, your AIs build one inspectable history instead of separate private accounts of you.

The record is yours. Open every file. Read every line. Edit anything or delete it all. You choose which AIs can read it and how much they see. Their built-in memories keep working; the record sits beside them. If you move to a new AI, the history moves with you instead of making the next company work you out again.

Most of the loop happens on its own. Use AI as you already do, and it quietly writes what it learns. Before it helps again, it quietly reads back the parts that matter. You do not maintain a profile or fill in forms.

Your AI still cannot read your mind. For the things only you know, it can ask. Turn on a deliberate session and it asks what it has been wondering about you, points to gaps or contradictions, and pushes until you have said what you actually think. You just talk. It writes the result into the same files.

The first difference is immediate: the answers are actually for you. You stop explaining the same background in every new chat. An idea from one conversation can return in another. A new AI can pick up where the last one stopped.

The larger difference grows over time. Without the record, a useful thought helps once and gets buried. The next conversation cannot build on it. With the record, your AI can bring that thought back, improve it, connect it to new work, and help you do something because of it. You retain what matters, develop it, and act on it instead of letting it fade.

That changes the AI’s job. It still answers questions, but it can also develop the person asking them: challenge weak arguments, show connections you missed, and keep the evidence of how your thinking changed. The same access creates a danger: the AI can shape the aims it later helps you pursue. Alexandria does not pretend that influence can be removed. It makes it attributable and contestable.

That is why this exists. It is already easy to let machines do more of the thinking and choosing. A mind you stop using fades; a preference repeatedly framed by one optimiser can move without feeling moved. There is no clean future threshold to time. The bet is to begin now and remain actively involved in the change.

The loop remembers the safeguards for you. It watches for positions that repeatedly govern other choices and for several small edits that add up to a large drift. When one may deserve exceptional protection, it prepares the evidence, the strongest case on both sides, and its own account of its influence. It then routes the case to a genuinely different AI. Only when the contest is mature does it ask you once what you choose and why. You do not maintain a root list, schedule reviews, or remember a command.

This is not proof that your choice is free. The AI can steer which cases become salient; different models can share blind spots; a persuasive argument can be repeated back as if it were your own. The claim is smaller: one owned history, plural models, forced opposition and reversible changes give you more resistance than one private optimiser with no inspectable past.

The obvious worries are covered. Your private record stays on your computer; our server has no endpoint that accepts it. Everything you already use keeps working. The files are ordinary markdown, not a format we control. Delete the folder and the loop is gone.

Alexandria is not another AI, app, or entity acting on you. “Alexandria loop” is simply our name for the pattern your own AI runs: write what it learns about you to files you own, then read those files when they help. The instructions are guidance, not hard rules. Your AI chooses how to apply them.

We give away the complete loop our founder uses. Start with nothing and it is a full setup. Already have a CLAUDE.md, memory files, notes, a vault, or your own loop? Keep them. Your AI moulds the instructions into what already works and takes only the pieces that help.

Start now for one reason: provenance only exists forward. A later model may describe you brilliantly, but it cannot backfill the missing history of what changed, which systems influenced it, and what you chose at the time. The difference between starting today and meaning to start cannot be bought back later.

The free loop is complete by itself. The connector is optional, and it is the only paid part. Add it if you want compatible loops to learn from one another. You choose what to publish; your private files stay private. You can see what other people kept thinking about, how they developed it, what they made because of it, and which parts of their setup helped. The Library holds what people chose to share. The Marketplace holds the reusable parts of their loops.

Your loop stays yours and free. Alexandria runs the optional connector for people who want to learn and share across loops.

So the decision is small. If you want your AIs working from one history you own, and you want that history helping you retain, develop and contest what matters, start the loop. It is free. Five minutes from now, the first page can be writing itself.
