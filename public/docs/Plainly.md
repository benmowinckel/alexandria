# alexandria, plainly.

The better your ai knows you, the better it helps you. When it remembers your job, or the project you’re working on, or how you like your answers, everything gets easier. Every ai company knows this. It’s why they’re all building memory into their products.

Now imagine that, a hundred times deeper. Not a few saved facts about your life: a complete written map of how you think. What you believe, how you reason, what you care about, the questions you keep coming back to. In one place, owned by you, read by every ai you choose. (In technical terms: unified personal context, sovereign, full depth.)

The built-in memories will keep getting better. Maybe they’ll reach this depth someday, maybe not. You don’t have to wait to find out. You can have this today, free, and it takes about five minutes.

Here’s how. We wrote a package of instructions that you paste into whatever ai you already use. They tell it to make a folder on your computer, and to write down what it learns about your thinking as you talk, in as much detail as it can. Piece by piece, your ai builds the most detailed map of you that has ever existed. A mirror of your mind.

And the map is yours, completely. It lives on your computer, in files you can open: read every line, edit anything, delete it all. You choose which ais read it and how much they see. The built-in memories keep working as before; this sits alongside them. And if you ever move to a new ai, the map moves with you, whole. (Access is yours to grant and revoke, per ai, per section. Unlike exported transcripts, the map is the understanding itself: nothing to re-derive.)

Most of it happens on its own. Your ai quietly writes as you use it, and quietly reads back whatever helps it answer you better. It never needs you to do anything. (The passive half of the loop.)

But it can’t read your mind. So for what it can’t see, it asks. Your ai gets a mode you can turn on where it becomes a kind of biographer: it asks what it’s been wondering about you, raises gaps and contradictions, wants your take on new things. You just talk. It pushes back until you’ve said what you actually think, then writes that down too. Most people have never once put their thinking into words, end to end. You’ll have it in writing. (The active half: deliberate drawing-out, and reading back on request.)

The first thing you get: answers that are actually for you. Talking to a stranger versus your closest friend of twenty years. You never re-explain yourself. It shows you things you’d genuinely love. And it compounds: the models get smarter every month, and all that new intelligence aims itself at you. An ai that knows you deeply today will be unbelievable in a year.

The second thing is bigger: what it does to you. Ai can hand you all the answers, and if that’s all it does, you slowly stop thinking, because something else is doing it for you. The map changes your ai’s job. It knows where your thinking stands, and it starts developing it: pushing your ideas further, showing you connections you didn’t see, bringing you new things worth thinking about. You still get every answer. You also stay someone who can do something original with them.

That second thing is why the company exists. It’s about to get very easy to never think again, and a mind you stop using fades. We built a company on the opposite: people keep thinking, and become more themselves, while everything around them gets automated. That’s why we give it away.

The worries are covered. No new exposure: you already tell your ai things, this only changes where the record lives. No server of ours: the files are on your computer, and we never see a word. Nothing replaced: everything you have keeps working, this plugs in alongside. No trap: it’s free, and deleting the folder makes it like it never happened.

And it isn’t software. No app, nothing to install: instructions your ai reads, then decides for itself what to take. What they describe is a pattern: your ai, reading and writing your thinking, in private files you own, keeping the mirror accurate and developing the mind it mirrors. That pattern is called an Alexandria loop, and it doesn’t belong to us. Some people already run one and have never heard of us; theirs counts just as much. We just make the pattern official, so anyone can have one. No loop? We give you a complete one. Half-open? We close it. Complete already? We make it better. Loops are the only thing this company does, so we can probably improve yours a little. (The test: your ai writes on its own and when you think out loud with it, reads on its own and on request, over files you own. All four running, and your loop is complete. Ours ships as plain markdown; any substrate qualifies.)

Start now rather than someday, for one reason: the map takes time, and it compounds. A mirror of a mind isn’t written in a weekend, and its value grows with every model release. The difference between starting today and meaning to can’t be bought back later, because the map is made of time spent thinking with it.

The company itself is deliberately small. Imagine Strava, except Strava also mailed free running shoes to everyone even slightly interested in running. Yours forever, no strings. Strava’s product was never shoes; it’s connecting runners. That’s us. The loop is the shoes, free because instructions cost nothing to copy. What we run is the connector: a small piece you click into your loop when you want it, linking you to everyone else who runs one. Copy the clever parts of other people’s setups, share yours. Show what your thinking produced, explore what everyone else made. A growing collection of written minds, each owned by its author. That’s the name: Alexandria, a library of human minds. The connector is the only thing we ever charge for; the shoes stay free either way. (The connector: an account, your published files, the call that links loops. The library holds what minds make; the marketplace, how loops are built.)

Step back and it’s simple. Everything else about you already has its tools; the one part with nothing behind it is the part that matters most, how you think. It was left open for a reason: a map of your thinking can’t be harvested from the outside. It only exists if you choose to make one. The technology was never the hard part. The choice is. So the company’s job is saying why, one person at a time, and connecting everyone who said yes. We exist so people keep thinking, and stay valuable through what’s coming.
